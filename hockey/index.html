Why edges are your competitive edge

Edge quality isn't a figure skating concept — it's the mechanical foundation of every elite hockey skill. Stephanie's assessment system identifies exactly where your edge efficiency is costing you on the ice.

Crossover Efficiency

Underpowered crossovers almost always trace back to outside-edge depth on the push leg. Fix the edge, get immediate return on every crossover stride.

Tight Turn Radius

Cutting tight at speed requires inside-edge commitment most players never practice in isolation. Vague edges at full speed mean a wider turn — and a half-step that's the difference between a check and a chance.

Backward Edge Confidence

Backward skating weaknesses — drifting, early pivots, tentative crossovers — almost always stem from under-loaded outside edges. Coaches and scouts see it immediately in positioning and gap control.

Pivot Speed

Forward-to-backward pivots and transitions rely on a single committed edge moment. Improve that edge, and transition speed improves with it — no additional speed training needed.

Acceleration

First-step and short-burst acceleration are determined largely by how much ice each push stroke grips. Edge depth directly controls grip. More grip, more acceleration.

Compact Stride

The tight, efficient stride that scouts want comes from a specific edge sequence through the full push cycle. Stephanie breaks that sequence down, finds the gap, and rebuilds it.
